# Lumper Backstop — Sourced Statistics

Every number below is traced to a named organization, a year, and a public URL. Figures we could **not** verify against a primary source are marked ⚠️ **UNVERIFIED — do not put on a slide.**

Last checked: 2026-08-24.

## 1. Driver detention costs

### The canonical federal number (DOT OIG, 2018)

- **$1.1 billion to $1.3 billion** in lost annual driver earnings. Exact wording: "detention is associated with reductions in annual earnings of **$1.1 billion to $1.3 billion** for for-hire commercial motor vehicle drivers in the truckload sector." — US DOT Office of Inspector General, report **ST2018019**, *Estimates Show Commercial Driver Detention Increases Crash Risks and Costs, but Current Data Limit Further Analysis*, **January 31, 2018**. [OIG library item](https://www.oig.dot.gov/library-item/36237) · [full PDF](https://www.oig.dot.gov/sites/default/files/FMCSA%20Driver%20Detention%20Final%20Report.pdf)
- **$250.6M–$302.9M** carrier net income. "detention reduces net income by **$250.6 million to $302.9 million** annually" for truckload motor carriers. — same report, [DOT OIG, 2018](https://www.oig.dot.gov/library-item/36237)
- **+6.2% crash rate.** "a 15-minute increase in average dwell time—the total time spent by a truck at a facility—increases the average expected crash rate by **6.2 percent**." — same report, [DOT OIG, 2018](https://www.oig.dot.gov/library-item/36237)
- **Per-driver breakdown of the OIG figure:** detention "reduc[es] the annual earnings per driver between **$1,281 and $1,534 per year**—a reduction between **3.0 and 3.6 percent**," for a "total net loss of **$1.35 to $1.60 billion** for the trucking industry overall." Also: the 6.2% crash increase resulted in "an additional **6,509 crashes per year**," and "every 5-percentage point increase in the number of stops experiencing detention … increased the crash risk by **4.7 percent**." — OOIDA Foundation, *2023 Detention Time Survey*, **February 2024**, restating OIG. [PDF](https://www.ooida.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/2023-Detention-Time-Survey-FINAL.pdf)

### The current number (ATRI, 2024)

Report: **American Transportation Research Institute (ATRI)**, *Costs and Consequences of Truck Driver Detention: A Comprehensive Analysis*, **September 10, 2024** (2023 data). The report PDF is gated behind a registration form; the figures below come from ATRI's own press release and trade-press coverage of it.

- **$15.1 billion total.** "the trucking industry lost **$3.6 billion in direct expenses** and **$11.5 billion in lost productivity** from driver detention in 2023." — [ATRI press release, Sept 10, 2024](https://truckingresearch.org/2024/09/new-research-documents-substantial-financial-and-safety-impacts-from-truck-driver-detention/)
- **39.3% of all stops.** "In 2023, drivers reported being detained in **39.3 percent** of all stops." Higher for women drivers (**49.1%**), refrigerated drivers (**56.2%**), and spot-market fleets (**42.5%**). — [ATRI, 2024](https://truckingresearch.org/2024/09/new-research-documents-substantial-financial-and-safety-impacts-from-truck-driver-detention/)
- **1 in 10 stops runs long.** "In **9.9%** of stops – one in every 10 – drivers experienced more than two hours of detention." — [Heavy Duty Trucking coverage of ATRI, Sept 10, 2024](https://www.truckinginfo.com/news/truck-driver-detention-study-shows-some-improvement)
- **Billed but not paid.** "**94.5 percent** of fleets charge detention fees, [but] they are paid for **fewer than 50 percent** of those invoices." — [ATRI, 2024](https://truckingresearch.org/2024/09/new-research-documents-substantial-financial-and-safety-impacts-from-truck-driver-detention/)
- **135+ million hours** of productive time lost by for-hire trucking in 2023 — roughly "15 days' worth of hours-of-service time per driver" and "about **7,000 miles**" per driver. Annual revenue loss per driver: **$11,000 to $19,000**. — [ATRI via Land Line, 2024](https://landline.media/study-shines-a-spotlight-on-costly-cascading-effects-of-detention-time/)
- **Safety knock-on:** "Trucks that were detained drove **14.6 percent faster** on average than trucks that were not detained." — [ATRI, 2024](https://truckingresearch.org/2024/09/new-research-documents-substantial-financial-and-safety-impacts-from-truck-driver-detention/)
- ⚠️ **Discrepancy to be aware of.** Land Line's coverage of the same ATRI report says detention was invoiced on "about **75%**" of eligible events with "a little over half" paid, while ATRI's own release says **94.5%** of fleets charge and fewer than 50% of invoices are paid. These measure different things (share of fleets vs. share of events). **Use ATRI's own release wording on stage.**
- ⚠️ **UNVERIFIED:** FMCSA's own study, *Impact of Driver Detention Time on Safety and Operations* (~80 carriers, ~2,500 drivers, ELD/telematics data), was slated for a **July 2025** final report. We could **not** confirm the final report has been published or find any published findings. Do not cite FMCSA detention numbers.

## 2. Detention mechanics — the free window and the hourly rate

- **Free window is 2 hours.** The DOT OIG defines detention as "the time spent at a facility **in excess of 2-hours**." — [DOT OIG 2018, as quoted by OOIDA Foundation, Feb 2024](https://www.ooida.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/2023-Detention-Time-Survey-FINAL.pdf)
- **Two thirds of carriers use the 2-hour mark.** "**67%** of carriers that charge detention fees do so starting at the two-hour mark." — [ATRI 2024, via Heavy Duty Trucking](https://www.truckinginfo.com/news/truck-driver-detention-study-shows-some-improvement)
- **$63.71/hour** — "average detention fee" per hour charged by fleets. — ATRI, driver detention study, **September 4, 2019**. [PR Newswire release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/new-atri-study-quantifies-driver-detention-impacts-300911706.html) — *note: this is a 2019 figure, not 2024. Cite the year.*
- **What owner-operators actually collect:** those under their own authority "collect about **$53 an hour** on average (a **$10 increase from 2022**), leased-on owner-operators receive **$45**" — "far from the hourly rate that members consider to be fair, which is approximately **$80**." — [OOIDA Foundation, *2023 Detention Time Survey*, Feb 2024](https://www.ooida.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/2023-Detention-Time-Survey-FINAL.pdf)
- **The wait itself:** "the average weekly wait time for loading is **7.2 hours**, and the average weekly wait time for unloading is **7.1 hours**, for a total of **14.3 hours**" per week. — [OOIDA Foundation, 2024](https://www.ooida.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/2023-Detention-Time-Survey-FINAL.pdf)
- **Opportunity cost of one dock hour:** "an owner-operator could potentially lose **$106.20 per hour** waiting to load or unload their truck (45 mph × 1 hour = 45 miles × $2.36 per mile)." — [OOIDA Foundation, 2024](https://www.ooida.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/2023-Detention-Time-Survey-FINAL.pdf)
- **Detention costs loads, not just hours:** "**50 percent** of respondents stated that they lose **one to two loads per week** on average because of detention, while another **13 percent** lose 3 or more." — [OOIDA Foundation, 2024](https://www.ooida.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/2023-Detention-Time-Survey-FINAL.pdf)
- ⚠️ **Soft number:** Land Line's ATRI coverage cites an "average detention fee" of **$50 per hour** after the 2-hour mark, which conflicts with ATRI's own 2019 **$63.71**. Blog/marketing sources widely quote a "$25–$100/hr, typically $50–$75" range with no underlying study. **Do not present a range as sourced.** Safest on-stage framing: *2-hour free window, roughly $50–$65/hour once it starts, paid on fewer than half the invoices.*

## 3. Freight/cargo fraud and double-brokering losses (US)

### Cargo theft totals

- **$725 million in 2025, up 60%.** "Cargo Theft Losses Surge to Estimated **$725 Million** in 2025" — nearly $725M, a **60% increase from 2024**. Verisk **CargoNet** recorded **3,594** supply-chain crime incidents (vs. 3,607 in 2024), of which **confirmed cargo theft rose 18%** from 2,243 to **2,646** incidents. Average value per theft rose **36% to $273,990** (from $202,364). — Verisk / CargoNet, published **January 21, 2026**. [CargoNet](https://www.cargonet.com/news-and-events/cargonet-in-the-media/2025-theft-trends/) · [Verisk newsroom](https://www.verisk.com/company/newsroom/cargo-theft-losses-surge-to-estimated-$725-million-in-2025-verisk-cargonet-analysis-reveals/)
- **2,576 US incidents in 2025, +16% YoY.** Deceptive-pickup incidents "rose **35% year over year and now represent 10%** of recorded cargo theft events"; pilferage remains the most common type at **43%**. — **Overhaul**, 2025 US cargo theft report, via [FreightWaves, Feb 18, 2026](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/california-texas-account-for-58-of-us-cargo-theft-in-2025)
- **"Up to $35 billion per year."** "Thieves targeting freight shipments cost the American economy **up to $35 billion per year**." Also: "Strategic theft has risen **1,500%** since the first quarter of 2021"; "The average value per theft is more than **$200,000**"; "There were **505** cargo theft incidents reported during the first quarter of this year — a **36% increase** compared with Q1 2024." — **American Trucking Associations**, *Freight Under Fire: The Explosive Rise of Cargo Theft*, **June 20, 2025**. [ATA](https://www.trucking.org/news-insights/freight-under-fire-explosive-rise-cargo-theft)
  - ⚠️ **Caveat on $35B.** This is a widely repeated round number that trade press attributes variously to NICB and DHS. We could not locate a primary methodology document behind it. ATA states it as "**up to** $35 billion," and **that hedged wording is how it should be said on stage**, attributed to ATA (2025).

### Double brokering and identity fraud

- **Unlawful brokerage is the #1 fraud tactic.** "**34%** of respondents cited this scheme" as the top fraud tactic. TIA Watchdog recorded "over **1,600 fraud reports** between September 2024 and February 2025—a staggering **65% increase** from the previous period." "**22%** of respondents reporting more than **$200,000** lost due to fraud in **just the past six months**"; "**10%** of respondents said they've spent over $200,000 on fraud prevention"; "**83%** of respondents experienced **at least three types of fraud** in the last six months." — **Transportation Intermediaries Association (TIA)**, *State of Fraud in the Industry* report, released **April 28, 2025**. [TIA](https://news.tianet.org/tia-releases-state-of-fraud-in-the-industry-april-2025-report/)
- **Fraud attempts at platform scale.** **Highway**, *Freight Fraud Index*:
  - Q1 2025: "blocking over **400,000 fraud attempts** in Q1 of 2025 alone" — **352,134** fraudulent inbound emails, **30,921** spoofed phone numbers, **561** user access attempts from 42 countries outside North America. Unlawful brokerage "has emerged as the **No. 1 threat**," with **402** reported incidents over six months. Published **May 12, 2025**. [Highway](https://highway.com/press-releases/highway-releases-freight-fraud-index-revealing-400-000-sophisticated-fraud-attempts-in-q1-2025)
  - Q3 2025: **605,728** fraudulent email attempts blocked in Q3; **62,531** fraudulent phone numbers; **2,992** broker identity alerts; **149** unauthorized FMCSA contact changes. **1,453,129** fraudulent emails blocked through the first three quarters of 2025 — already exceeding the **full-year 2024 total of 914,719**. Published **October 27, 2025**. [Highway Q3 2025 Freight Fraud Index](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/highway-releases-q3-2025-freight-130800240.html)
- **Double brokering, sized.** "the amount of freight that would be 'by-the-letter double brokering' is about **$500 million to $700 million per year**." — Garrett Wolfe, Chief Strategy Officer, **TriumphPay**, via [FreightWaves, April 24, 2023](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/tias-stark-message-double-brokering-fraud-out-of-control). ⚠️ **Read the wording carefully:** this is the **value of freight moved via double brokering**, *not* dollars lost. Do not restate it as "losses."
- ⚠️ **UNVERIFIED — "TIA estimates double brokering costs carriers $700 million to $1 billion annually."** This appears in aggregator/blog content but we found **no TIA publication** stating it. Do not use.
- ⚠️ **UNVERIFIED — "$4 billion in double brokering losses between 2022 and 2025"** and **"15,419 broker revocations / $10 billion total industry losses."** Only found on low-quality SEO/blog pages with no primary attribution. Do not use.
- ⚠️ **UNVERIFIED — "FMCSA received over 8,000 double brokering complaints in 2025, up from roughly 2,000 in 2021."** Repeated in trade/blog coverage; no FMCSA primary located. Do not use.

## 4. Global cargo theft

Primary source: **BSI Consulting and TT Club 2025 Cargo Theft Report**, published **April 2026** (covering calendar 2025). [Full PDF](https://www.ttclub.com/fileadmin/uploads/tt-club/Documents/Corporate_Literature/TT_Club_BSI_2025_Cargo_Theft_Report_FINAL.pdf) · [BSI press release, April 21, 2026](https://www.bsigroup.com/en-US/insights-and-media/media-center/press-releases/2026/april/tt-club-and-bsi-report-rise-in-cargo-theft-as-criminal-tactics-evolve/)

- ⚠️ **There is no global dollar total.** We read the full 16-page report. It contains **no worldwide loss figure**. The only dollar amounts in it are UK-specific ("losses reaching **149 million USD in 2024**") and one incident ("the **nine million USD** smartphone heist at Heathrow"). **Do not claim a global cargo-theft dollar number sourced to BSI/TT Club.**
- **Trucks are the target.** "Trucks remained the dominant modality for cargo theft, representing roughly **three-quarters** of all cases" globally (the press release says **70%**). "**22%** of cargo theft cases worldwide" involved insider cooperation. — [BSI/TT Club, 2026](https://www.ttclub.com/fileadmin/uploads/tt-club/Documents/Corporate_Literature/TT_Club_BSI_2025_Cargo_Theft_Report_FINAL.pdf)
- **US theft-type mix explicitly names double brokering.** In the US, "Theft overwhelmingly targeted trucks (**67%**), followed by facilities (**16%**) and rail (**10%**), with the most frequent theft types including **fictitious pickups**, theft from vehicle, vehicle theft, container and trailer theft, **double and triple brokering**, and theft from facilities." California **31%**, Texas **15%**. Insider involvement "remained comparatively low at **6%**," but "**doubled from the previous year**." — [BSI/TT Club, 2026](https://www.ttclub.com/fileadmin/uploads/tt-club/Documents/Corporate_Literature/TT_Club_BSI_2025_Cargo_Theft_Report_FINAL.pdf)
- **Load boards are the attack surface.** "An estimated **75–80% of shipments are now directed toward load boards**, not only as a last resort but as a routine method of securing capacity." The report devotes a full section to load boards, noting "the increasing sophistication of criminal activity, particularly through the use of **data scraping, artificial intelligence, and impersonation techniques**." — [BSI/TT Club, 2026](https://www.ttclub.com/fileadmin/uploads/tt-club/Documents/Corporate_Literature/TT_Club_BSI_2025_Cargo_Theft_Report_FINAL.pdf)
- ⚠️ **UNVERIFIED — "North American losses reached $6.6 billion in 2025."** This circulates as if it were a BSI/TT Club finding. It is **not in their report**. Insurance Business attributes it to **Geotab**: "Separate data from **Geotab** pegged North American losses at **$6.6 billion** in 2025, with incidents up 18% year-on-year and average values climbing 36% to roughly $274,000" ([Insurance Business, April 21, 2026](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/breaking-news/global-cargo-theft-hits-new-highs-as-criminal-networks-evolve--tt-club-572468.aspx)). The "+18%" and "+36% to ~$274,000" match CargoNet's 2025 figures exactly, so the $6.6B looks like a derived extrapolation. **No Geotab primary located. Do not use.**

## 5. Scale context — how fragmented US trucking is

Primary source: **American Trucking Associations**, *American Trucking Trends 2025*, published **August 28, 2025** (2024 data). [ATA Trends 2025](https://www.trucking.org/news-insights/ata-american-trucking-trends-2025) · [ATA Economics and Industry Data](https://www.trucking.org/economics-and-industry-data)

- **~580,000 carriers.** "almost **580,000 active US motor carriers** registered with FMCSA that own or lease at least one tractor," as of **June 2025**. — [ATA, 2025](https://www.trucking.org/economics-and-industry-data)
- **91.5% run 10 trucks or fewer.** "**91.5%** operate 10 or fewer trucks" and "**99.3%** operate 100 or fewer trucks." — [ATA, 2025](https://www.trucking.org/economics-and-industry-data)
- **3.58 million drivers.** "Trucking employed **8.4 million** people in industry-related jobs, including **3.58 million professional drivers** in 2024." — [ATA Trends 2025](https://www.trucking.org/news-insights/ata-american-trucking-trends-2025)
- **$906 billion** in gross freight revenues (2024). — [ATA, 2025](https://www.trucking.org/economics-and-industry-data)
- **Broader FMCSA register (different denominator):** **2,113,851** registered motor carriers, **9,483,664** drivers, **8,538,324** vehicles, as of **July 31, 2026**. — [FMCSA Analysis & Information Online, Registration Statistics](https://ai.fmcsa.dot.gov/RegistrationStatistics). *This count includes private and intrastate carriers, so it is much larger than ATA's ~580,000 tractor-owning figure. Don't mix the two.*
- ⚠️ **Note on the popular "95.8%" stat.** ATA's 2023 Trends edition (2022 data) said **95.8%** of fleets operated 10 or fewer trucks. The current edition says **91.5%**. **Use 91.5% (ATA, 2025)**; 95.8% is stale.
- ⚠️ **UNVERIFIED — "nearly 65% of for-hire trucking companies have just one power unit"** and **"922,854 independent owner-operators."** Both circulate widely but we found only blog-tier sourcing; FMCSA's public registration page does not publish a fleet-size distribution. Do not use.

## Numbers we can say on stage

Four defensible one-liners, each traceable to a named org and year:

1. **"Detention costs truck drivers $1.1 to $1.3 billion in lost earnings every year — that's the US DOT Inspector General's own number, from 2018."**
   [DOT OIG, report ST2018019, Jan 31, 2018](https://www.oig.dot.gov/library-item/36237)

2. **"ATRI put the 2023 bill at $15.1 billion — $3.6 billion in direct expenses plus $11.5 billion in lost productivity. Drivers were detained on 39.3% of all stops, and even though 94.5% of fleets bill for it, fewer than half those invoices get paid."**
   [ATRI, *Costs and Consequences of Truck Driver Detention*, Sept 10, 2024](https://truckingresearch.org/2024/09/new-research-documents-substantial-financial-and-safety-impacts-from-truck-driver-detention/)

3. **"US cargo theft losses hit an estimated $725 million in 2025 — up 60% in one year — with the average theft now worth $273,990."**
   [Verisk CargoNet, Jan 21, 2026](https://www.cargonet.com/news-and-events/cargonet-in-the-media/2025-theft-trends/)

4. **"75 to 80% of US shipments now go through load boards — and TT Club and BSI name double and triple brokering and fictitious pickups among the most frequent US theft types. That's the front door we're guarding."**
   [BSI Consulting & TT Club 2025 Cargo Theft Report, April 2026](https://www.ttclub.com/fileadmin/uploads/tt-club/Documents/Corporate_Literature/TT_Club_BSI_2025_Cargo_Theft_Report_FINAL.pdf)

**Bonus, if a judge asks "how big is the market?":**
> "~580,000 active US motor carriers own or lease at least one tractor, and 91.5% of them run 10 trucks or fewer. This is a small-business industry." — [ATA, 2025](https://www.trucking.org/economics-and-industry-data)

**Say "up to" if you use $35 billion.** ATA's exact phrasing is "up to $35 billion per year," and the underlying methodology is not public. [ATA, June 20, 2025](https://www.trucking.org/news-insights/freight-under-fire-explosive-rise-cargo-theft)
