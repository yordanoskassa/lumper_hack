# Sentinel — Story, Character, Script

---

## 1. The character

**Victor Amaya — 41. Colombian-American. Amaya Freight LLC. MC-1129043. One truck.**

Ten years driving for a fleet out of Columbus before he signed for his own tractor:
a 2019 Cascadia, **$2,180 a month, 51 payments left**.

He closed on his first home fourteen months ago — the same quarter he bought the
truck, because that was the first time the numbers looked possible. Now he has one
truck, one new mortgage, and no room for a bad month.

### The month, before a single gallon of diesel

| | |
|---|---|
| Mortgage | **$2,640** |
| Truck note | **$2,180** |
| Commercial insurance | **$1,180** |
| Home taxes & utilities | **$520** |
| **Fixed, every month** | **$6,520** |

Victor's settlements land and leave just as fast. There is no version of this
month where the truck sits still. He is already struggling to make the numbers
work before anything goes wrong.

**Victor is not careless and he is not naive.** He is a business with one asset,
one driver, and no back office — negotiating against desks that have all three.
That asymmetry is the entire story.

---

## 2. The story

### Beat 1 — Year one: he bought a back office, and it ate him alive

He did it right at first. He paid for the infrastructure a carrier is supposed to have:

| | |
|---|---|
| Dispatcher, 10% of gross | **~$1,760/mo** |
| Factoring, 3% to get paid in 2 days instead of 45 | **~$530/mo** |
| Compliance & billing service | **$550/mo** |
| **Back office** | **~$2,840/mo** |

$2,840 a month to have someone else find the loads, check the brokers, chase the
invoices. On paper it's the cost of doing business. Against $6,520 of fixed cost
and a rate market that fell all year, it was the difference between a hard month
and an impossible one.

**Month nine, he cancelled all of it.** Dispatcher, factoring, service. Gone.

That was the only decision available. It was also the decision that got him robbed —
because the back office didn't disappear. It moved.

> **It became him. In a truck stop parking lot. At 11:47 PM. After eleven hours of driving.**

Finding tomorrow's load, checking who he's about to call, arguing an invoice —
on a phone, on four hours of sleep, at the exact hour a human being is worst at
noticing that something is off.

### Beat 2 — March. The load that looked perfect

Truck's empty in Toledo. Posting: **Toledo → Charlotte, 540 miles, $1,450.**
The broker's MC checks out — real authority, real insurance, operating since 2011.
He calls the number on the posting. Friendly guy, fast. Rate con in nine minutes.

He hauls it. Delivers clean. Invoices.

### Beat 3 — The money never comes

Day 30, nothing. Day 60, he calls the number on the rate con — **dead**.

So he calls the *real* broker. The one whose MC was on the posting.

They have never heard of Victor Amaya. **They already paid that load** — to the
person who took it from them and re-posted it under their name.

That is **double brokering**. Victor was never in contract with anyone who existed.
No bond claim, no lien, nobody to sue. **$4,000 gone** — that load and the one
before it from the same ring, which he'd taken *because the first one went fine.*

**The tell was on the screen the whole time.** The MC was real. The *phone number*
on the posting belonged to a stranger — not the registered contact for that MC.
One cross-check catches it. A dispatcher does that cross-check. Victor cancelled
the dispatcher in month nine, and at 11:47 PM he did not.

### Beat 4 — Then they take the waiting time too

May. Laredo. On the dock at **06:40** for an 07:00 appointment. They start loading
him at **13:15**. Six hours and thirty-five minutes, engine off, in the heat.

Two hours are free. The other **4.5 hours** are owed at $65/hr — **$292**.

He files. The broker denies it: *no proof of arrival time.* And they're right,
technically. Nobody stamped the arrival. Nobody sent written notice at the minute
the free window closed, which the contract requires. A real debt evaporates
because there was no evidence and no timestamp — because the back office that
would have sent that notice was a man asleep in a bunk.

### Beat 5 — The compounding

$4,292 that should have existed doesn't. The mortgage doesn't move. Victor takes
cheaper freight to catch up — thinner margin, no cushion — so the next time a
too-good posting appears, he takes it, because he needs it.

**Getting robbed is what makes you easy to rob again.**

### Beat 6 — The turn

Victor never needed a dashboard, an analytics suite, or a co-pilot.

**He needed the back office he could not afford.**

Four agents. Not eight — four, because each one owns a fight he was losing alone.

| Agent | Replaces | The fight |
|---|---|---|
| **Finder** | the dispatcher's search | Pulls every board, then proves the load clears a profit on real route miles, real regional diesel, real fixed cost. Widens its own radius when the board is thin. |
| **Verifier** | the dispatcher's phone call | Eight checks, three sources — including the **callback cross-check** that would have killed the March load before he ever dialed. |
| **Closer** | the dispatcher's follow-up | Anchors on lane comps, negotiates, then chases the broker who goes quiet on a bounded, escalating backoff. |
| **Payday** | the billing service + factoring | Geofences the dock, stamps arrival, sends the written detention notice at the exact minute the free window closes, staples GPS-in/GPS-out to the invoice, then ages it until it's paid. |

$2,840 a month of back office, running at 11:47 PM whether Victor is awake or not.

### Beat 7 — The payoff

October. A posting comes up. Good rate. Verifier screens it and comes back not
with a risk score, but with a sentence:

> **"Refuse. The ACH account on this rate con is the same account that took
> $4,000 off you on March 14th. Different MC. Same bank."**

Victor never had a memory. Now the business has one, and it does not forget a
bank account.

## 4. Direction notes

- **No voice-over. No narration captions.** The few words on screen are part of the composition.
- The opening starts empty and uses no bold type. Let the thin human drawing establish Victor first.
- Reveal language one word at a time. Alternate soft-focus, upward drift, and lateral movement.
- Animate the evidence too: totals count, clocks advance, cards enter, status trails resolve, and refusal states pulse.
- Let empty space and long holds carry the emotion. The numbers do the acting.
- **Show the refusal.** Everyone demos the happy path. Refusing a load is the money shot.
- Cab-dark `#1E1E1E` is *always* Victor's side. Paper `#FAFAFA` is *always* the system's.
  Never blend them — the cut between them is the argument.
- Every product claim on screen is something the app does live. No mockups.
- **Never say "AI-powered." Never say "revolutionize."**

**Numbers that carry the credibility:**
$6,520/mo fixed · $2,840/mo back office · month 9 · 11:47 PM · $4,000 · 540 mi · day 60 · 06:40→13:15 · $65/hr · $292

**One-sentence pitch:**
> A one-truck carrier cancelled his back office to make the mortgage, and got
> double-brokered and stiffed on detention because of it. Sentinel is that back
> office, rebuilt as four agents that never sleep.
